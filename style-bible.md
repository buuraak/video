# Style bible

What our films look like and why. Keep this to one page.

**The approved look:** `reference/style_v2.mp4` (with its contact sheet `reference/style_v2_sheet.png`). When in doubt, match it.

## World

Dark. Films are 1920×1080 at 30 frames per second.

## Lines

Everything is drawn with slightly wobbly, hand-drawn lines that are re-drawn every 3 frames, so they gently "boil".

## Colours

One accent colour: **red**. Red means failure.

Background near-black, lines pale chalk-white, quiet details grey, the enemy red. Screens (his phone) give off a cold blue-white light. Exact values live in `toolkit/style.py`.

## The character

- A simple stick figure that looks mostly hand drawn. Small, intentional inconsistencies are fine; they make him look more natural.
- Defining detail: at the start he is always tired and depressed. He has bags under his eyes and looks sad.
- Size on screen: medium to big. The viewer notices he's there, but he isn't huge.
- He faces the enemy, turned side-on, not the viewer. His tired head droops forward, toward the enemy.
- At the end of the grind he goes numb: no frown, flat brows, heavy lids (film 1, row 17).
- No red on his face. Sleeplessness shows through bags, heavy lids and worried brows only. Rejected (film 1): red rims round the eyes, red bags, red veins, a red glow under the eyes. In the dark, the red clock's light may fall faintly on him (row 19), never enough to make a red face.
- In close-ups he is still a stick figure: an arm is just a thick line, with no hand drawn.
- Lying on his back seen from above, he rests his hands on his belly. (Arms hanging from one point on his spine looked like a tent.)
- Seen from straight above, he is drawn truly from above: standing, we see the top of his head with arms and feet poking out as he walks; sitting up, his face turns away out of view.
- Seen from behind (row 20), he is the back of a plain round head above slumped shoulders.

## The enemy

- Mostly the same as the stick figure, but red.
- Bigger than the character. He is the largest character on screen.
- Looks evil and malicious, with an evil grin.
- His outlines glitch occasionally.
- He has a red, dark glow around him.
- He doesn't have to appear in every film. Film 1 (Tomorrow) has no enemy figure: the red clock is the enemy.

## Font

**Noteworthy Bold** (already on every Mac). Hand lettered to match the stick figure, but still easy to read. Rejected: Chalkboard SE and Marker Felt (too cartoonish), Chalkduster (hard to read), Bradley Hand (too thin), Avenir Next (too clean).

His own writing in the world (the whiteboard) uses it too, so the caption's voice reads as his.

## Other people and the outside

- **People in the background** (the joggers) are grey stick figures, so he stays the only pale figure and the eye stays on him (`sheets/jogger_options.png`). Rejected: pale with a cap, dark shapes. **Sam** is different: pale like him, with messy hair, so Sam reads as someone who matters (`sheets/sam_options.png`).
- **The street at dawn**, from the side: a row of dark buildings with nearly all windows dark, a streetlamp's cold pool of light, the pavement and the kerb, the sky a little lighter and greyer low down. The park is kept for the green payoff at the end.
- **Outside his door** (row 29): a path leading away between city buildings, in grey, rainy light (not blue).
- **The gym** (row 25), from the side: dark, a long mirror, a rack of dumbbells, a bench, a cold light overhead.

## Film 1's things

- **The whiteboard:** dark, with a faint shine, a frame and a marker tray: clearly a board, but as dark as the room. Pale writing; only the crossing-out is red (and, at the very end, the ticks are green, with a green marker on the tray). From FRIDAY on he cares less: each red slash is more crooked than the last and runs past the word. At rock bottom the days fill every gap and run over his goals, smaller and tilted, until the goals are nearly buried in red. After the turn the clean board holds TODAY and five easy things, each with an empty box, in pale marker. Rejected: outline only (doesn't read as a board), a real pale whiteboard (too bright).
- **Phone messages:** a real lock screen seen from above: the time big at the top, a pale rounded banner with a round initial, the sender's name and the message in dark writing. Rejected: a chat bubble, words glowing alone. Inside a chat (row 24): dark, Sam's bubbles grey on the left, his cold blue on the right.
- **His notebook:** a dark page with faint ruled lines and pale ink, like the board (`sheets/notebook_chat_options.png`).
- **The quote reel:** a smug stick-figure man in a suit (tie, lapels), and under him a dark caption box with the words in big bold capitals. Rejected: the words alone, a loud coach.
- **His empty bottles:** lying on their sides, one on the nightstand by the clock and one on the floor by the bed (from above, a standing bottle is just a circle and reads as a cup).
- **The discipline bar:** a hand-drawn outline labelled DISCIPLINE, centred in the top black bar, in every scene. A smooth pale fill slides up one equal step each time he does something. It flashes red as it drains, flickers red when already empty, and turns green only when full. Rejected: separate notches, coloured in with pen strokes (`sheets/discipline_options.png`).
- **The reset:** the red clock's numbers race backwards from 23:59, faster and faster with a fading red trail, and slam onto 6:00 with a camera jolt. Rejected: the numbers glitching and tearing.

## Captions

Bottom centre, inside the bottom black bar, so they never cover the drawing. Pale white, with words of failure in red. One exception (film 1, row 27): the last, biggest "I'll start *tomorrow*." fills the picture, the room darkened behind it.

## Finish

Light film grain, a soft vignette (darker corners), and black bars at the top and bottom that give a wide 2.35:1 cinema picture.

## Pace

Unhurried. It's a short animated film, not a trailer: every beat gets room to breathe, and the viewer should never feel rushed. Let actions finish, let the viewer read and take in faces. When in doubt, longer. The film's length can stretch to allow it.

## Camera movement

Shaky and handheld, as if someone is recording. A shot that "stays in the same position" still keeps this gentle wobble; it just doesn't move or zoom.

## The rule we never break

No positive colours until the end. A positive colour is, for example, green. Film 1's first green (row 30): the trees' leaves in the park, a soft muted green, and the discipline bar as it fills; everything else stays grey.

## Music

None until the payoff: film 1's first music is a warm, hopeful piano and strings piece (ElevenLabs) under the final run.
